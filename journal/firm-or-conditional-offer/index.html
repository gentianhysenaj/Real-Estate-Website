Firm or conditional: choosing the right offer

A condition protects the buyer, but it gives the seller less certainty. Knowing when to use one, and when to go firm, is one of the most important decisions in any deal.

What a condition is

A condition is a clause that lets the buyer walk away if something isn't satisfied by a set date. The most common are financing, a home inspection, a lawyer's review of a condo's status certificate, and the sale of the buyer's current home.

Why buyers want them

Conditions give you time to confirm your mortgage, find out about expensive repairs, or check a condo corporation's finances before you're fully committed. In a balanced or slower market, sellers often accept them.

Why sellers prefer firm offers

A firm offer means the deal is done, with no waiting and no risk of it falling apart. When several buyers are competing, a conditional offer usually has to be noticeably stronger on price to win against firm ones.

How buyers can go firm more safely

  • Get a solid pre-approval and talk to your lender about the specific property before offer day.
  • Book a pre-offer home inspection if the seller allows it.
  • For condos, have your lawyer review the status certificate before you offer.
  • Understand appraisal risk: if the lender values the home below your price, you cover the difference.

How sellers should compare offers

Look past the headline price. The deposit, the conditions, the closing date and the inclusions all matter, and a slightly lower firm offer can be worth more than a higher conditional one.

The bottom line

There's no single right answer. It depends on the home, the market that week and your own situation. Whichever side you're on, I'll help you weigh the risk before you sign anything.
