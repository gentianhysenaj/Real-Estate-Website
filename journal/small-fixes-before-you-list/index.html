Five small fixes that pay off before you list

Buyers decide within seconds whether a home feels cared for. These jobs cost little, take a weekend or two, and show up in every photo and showing.

1. Paint the rooms that need it

You don't have to repaint the whole house. Start with the rooms buyers see first and any walls with marks, bold colours or scuffed trim. A warm, soft white or a light greige photographs well and lets buyers picture their own furniture.

2. Get the lighting right

Replace every burnt-out bulb and use the same colour temperature throughout, ideally a warm white. Clean the windows, open every blind and add a lamp to dark corners. Bright rooms look bigger online and feel better in person.

3. Clean deeply and clear surfaces

Clear kitchen and bathroom counters, take down most personal photos and thin out closets so they look roomy. If it's hard to part with things, a short-term storage unit for a couple of months is often worth it. A professional deep clean, including grout and baseboards, is money well spent.

4. Fix the small things buyers notice

Dripping taps, sticky doors, loose handles, cracked switch plates and tired bathroom caulking all make buyers wonder what else has been missed. Most are quick jobs, and they also help when a home inspector comes through.

5. Make the front door count

The first photo is usually the outside. Cut the grass or clear the snow, tidy the garden beds, wash or repaint the front door, and add a new doormat and a plant or two. It sets the tone for everything after it.

What to skip

Large renovations right before listing rarely pay back in full, and they can delay your launch. Before you spend on anything bigger than paint, ask me. I'll walk through with you and give you a short list in order of what will matter most to buyers in your area.
