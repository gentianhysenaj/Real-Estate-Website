Buying your first home in the GTA, step by step

Buying your first home is a lot of steps, but they happen in a predictable order. Here's the path from saving to getting your keys, and what to watch for along the way.

1. Work out your real budget

Start with your down payment. In Canada the minimum is 5% of the first $500,000 of the price and 10% of the portion above that, and higher-priced homes need 20% down. On top of the down payment, plan for closing costs: land transfer tax (in the City of Toronto you pay both the provincial and the municipal tax), legal fees, title insurance and adjustments. First-time buyers may qualify for land transfer tax rebates.

2. Use the savings tools you're entitled to

A First Home Savings Account (FHSA) lets you save up to $8,000 a year, to a lifetime total of $40,000, with tax-deductible contributions and tax-free withdrawals for your first home. The RRSP Home Buyers' Plan lets you borrow from your own RRSP for a down payment. Rules change, so confirm the current limits with your financial advisor.

3. Get pre-approved

A mortgage pre-approval tells you how much a lender will likely lend and can hold an interest rate for a set period. Expect to qualify under the federal stress test, at a rate higher than the one you'll actually pay. A pre-approval is not a final approval, which matters when you make your offer.

4. Decide what matters and start looking

Write down your must-haves and your nice-to-haves, then pick a few neighbourhoods. For condos, look closely at the monthly fees and what they cover. I'll set you up with new listings as soon as they hit the market and give you an honest read at every showing.

5. Make an offer

Your offer covers price, deposit, closing date, inclusions and any conditions, such as financing, a home inspection or a review of a condo's status certificate. The deposit is usually due shortly after the offer is accepted, often within 24 hours. In busy markets there may be an offer date and competing bids, and we'll plan for that in advance.

6. From accepted offer to closing day

Once the deal is firm, your lender finalises the mortgage, you arrange home insurance, and your real estate lawyer handles the title search and paperwork. You'll usually get a final visit before closing. On closing day the funds move through the lawyers, and you pick up your keys.

Every purchase is a little different. If you're starting to think about buying, I'm happy to walk you through your numbers before you look at a single home.
